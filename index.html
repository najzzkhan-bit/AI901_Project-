Agent Security Surface

Delegating Real-World Authority: Structural Risks

Delegating real-world authority to autonomous systems introduces significant risks across execution pipelines, external integrations, and human cognition.

⚠️ Execution Risk

Cascading Autonomy

When agents have access to email, financial systems, or development environments, a reasoning error can trigger unintended actions across connected services in an uncontrolled cascade.

Safeguard: Strict API action boundaries, transactional rate limits, and mandatory human confirmation gates before state mutations.
🛡️ Security Risk

Prompt Injection & Hijacking

Agents processing untrusted web pages, documents, or emails can encounter malicious instructions designed to expose private data or trigger unauthorized tool calls.

Safeguard: Strict isolation of untrusted retrieved data from instruction contexts, heuristic content sanitization, and read-only sandboxes.
🧩 Cognitive Risk

Loss of Cognitive Agency

Over-reliance on AI for decision-making, creative work, and relationship maintenance may weaken independent judgment and reinforce algorithmic confirmation bias.

Safeguard: Auditable reasoning chains, transparent confidence scoring, and explicit design nudges that preserve independent human deliberation.
Governance & Auditability

Balancing Rapid Agent Deployment with Safety

Technical safeguards, effective governance, and appropriate human oversight required to govern autonomous systems.

Core Principle: Treating AI systems as unchecked authorities creates operational and psychological vulnerabilities. The optimal approach treats AI agents as powerful, auditable tools—maximizing productivity and support while maintaining explicit boundaries, human oversight, and verifiable safety controls.
